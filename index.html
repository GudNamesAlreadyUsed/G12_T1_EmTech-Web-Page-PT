Bantay-Isip: Stopping School Violence at the Root

Overview

When fights, stabbings, or shootings happen on school grounds, the first response is always to call the police, search backpacks, and put more guards at the gate. But guards only catch a weapon after someone has already decided to hurt a classmate. Real safety starts with understanding what breaks inside a student long before they snap.

Campaign Mission

Classroom violence is rarely a random act. It is almost always the boiling point of untreated depression, violent bullying, domestic trauma, and an overwhelming shortage of guidance counselors who could have stepped in months earlier.

This campaign breaks down the connection between student mental health struggles and recent campus violence across the country, highlighting what happened right here in Las Piñas and providing direct steps and hotlines to help students before another tragedy occurs.

01

Why Aggression Replaces Communication

Teenagers dealing with chronic family trauma, anxiety, or depression rarely have the words or skills to process emotional pain alone. When there are no safe outlets to talk, bottled-up frustration often turns outward into defiance, verbal attacks, and physical violence against peers.

02

The Reality of Bullying in Philippine Schools

Reports from EDCOM II and international PISA assessments show that 1 in 3 Filipino students experiences regular bullying. Today, bullying does not stop at the school gate; it follows students home through anonymous messages, smear posts, and group chats 24/7. When victims feel trapped and see no intervention from school authorities, some turn to retaliatory violence as their only way out.

03

The Guidance Counselor Shortage

Across the Philippines, there is a massive deficit of licensed guidance counselors, with over 40,000 public schools lacking dedicated mental health professionals. In most campuses, a regular subject teacher is assigned as a part-time guidance advocate for thousands of learners. Warning signs like self-harm, extreme withdrawal, and violent threats slip through the cracks every single day simply because there are not enough professionals on campus to catch them.

Legal Protections

National legislation and DepEd policies establishing protective frameworks, anti-bullying mandates, and mental health obligations for educational institutions.

RA 10627

Republic Act 10627 (Anti-Bullying Act of 2013)

Requires all elementary and high schools to maintain an active Child Protection Committee (CPC). Schools are legally obligated to investigate bullying reports, protect victims, and provide mandatory counseling for both victims and aggressors.

View Statutory Key Mandates ↓
  • Mandatory CPC Setup: Every campus must form a dedicated committee including school heads, teachers, parents, and student council reps.
  • Prohibition of Retaliation: Immediate sanctions against anyone who harasses or intimidates a reporting student or witness.
  • Cyberbullying Coverage: Extends disciplinary authority to online bullying occurring off-campus that disrupts the learning environment.
RA 11036

Republic Act 11036 (Philippine Mental Health Act)

Mandates educational institutions to integrate age-appropriate mental health programs, reduce stigma, and provide accessible psychosocial support services for all learners.

View Statutory Key Mandates ↓
  • Psychosocial Support Services: Schools must establish accessible referral systems to licensed psychologists and psychiatrists.
  • Anti-Discrimination Safeguards: Explicit protection against academic expulsion or suspension purely based on mental health conditions.
  • Teacher Crisis Training: Periodic mental health first-aid workshops for faculty to recognize early warning signs.
DO 40

DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012 (Child Protection Policy)

Enforces zero tolerance for any form of child abuse, physical aggression, exploitation, or harassment within school premises.

View Statutory Key Mandates ↓
  • Strict Zero Tolerance: Prohibits corporal punishment, verbal abuse, or humiliation by teachers or peers.
  • Expedited Investigation: Obligates school heads to begin immediate inquiries within 48 hours of any reported incident.
  • Confidentiality Protection: Strict data privacy safeguarding the identities of victims and reporting parties.

Action Steps for Students

01

Stop recording and sharing fights.

Cheering on violence or posting brawl videos on social media platforms fuels aggression and violates digital ethics.

02

Do not provoke someone who is having an emotional breakdown.

Step back, give them space, and de-escalate the situation.

03

Report threats and weapons immediately.

If a classmate talks about wanting to die, threatens someone, or brings a weapon to school, notify a teacher, guidance advocate, or school head right away.

How We Built This (6 ICT Phases)

01

Picking the Advocacy

We wanted to address a real issue affecting Filipino students. After discussing recent campus incidents, our group chose to focus on student mental health and campus violence. We created our Messenger group chat to coordinate and brainstorm our initial ideas.

02

Setting the Scope & Roles

We divided the website into 6 clear sections (Home, Recent Incidents, Mental Health Link, Laws, Hotlines, and About Us). We assigned roles to each member in our group chat so research, design, and coding could happen at the same time.

03

Design & Layout

We drafted wireframes using a dark editorial magazine aesthetic. We picked clean typography (Outfit and Plus Jakarta Sans), high-contrast colors (teal and soft crimson), and prioritized mobile usability so navigation is effortless on phones.

04

Workspace & Asset Setup

We set up our GitHub repository, organized our project folder structure, and created a shared Google Drive folder where members could drop website assets, banners, and code files.

05

Coding & Interactive Features

We built the site with semantic HTML5, vanilla CSS, and JavaScript without heavy frameworks. We added live search filtering, smooth tab transitions, copy buttons, and an interactive 4-7-8 breathing tool.

06

Testing & Final Review

We tested the site on multiple mobile phones and desktop screens, checked all telephone links, proofread our text, compiled our screenshot evidence, and finalized the project for submission.
