Bantay-Isip Stopping School Violence at the Root

Overview

When fights, stabbings, or shootings happen on school grounds, the first response is always to call the police, search backpacks, and put more guards at the gate. But guards only catch a weapon after someone has already decided to hurt a classmate. Real safety starts with understanding what breaks inside a student long before they snap.

Campaign Mission

Violence on school grounds is never a random surprise. It is almost always the breaking point of quiet depression, relentless bullying, home troubles, and a severe lack of guidance counselors who could have intervened months earlier.

Bantay-Isip is our student-led advocacy. We want to show what connects mental health struggles to campus violence — starting right here in our city of Las Piñas — and give students, teachers, and parents real action steps and trusted hotlines before another life is lost.

The Root Causes

Why hurt students hurt others, what our schools are missing, and why talking early stops things from going too far.

Feeling Overwhelmed or Angry?

If your chest feels tight or you're about to snap, take a minute with our 4-7-8 breathing exercise.

01

Why Hurt Turns Into Anger

When a student is dealing with family problems, constant stress, or feeling worthless, it's hard to put that pain into words. Without a safe person to talk to, bottled-up hurt easily turns into shouting, fighting, and wanting to hurt someone else before they hurt you.

02

Bullying Follows You Everywhere

Reports from EDCOM II Findings ↗ and PISA assessments show that 1 in 3 Filipino students gets bullied regularly. Today, it doesn't end at 4 PM when the bell rings — it follows you into your DMs, comment sections, and group chats 24/7. When someone feels cornered with nowhere to escape, they eventually snap.

03

Not Enough Guidance Counselors

Across the Philippines, over 45,000 public schools share only around 1,000 licensed guidance counselors ↗. Most of the time, regular subject teachers have to juggle guidance work for thousands of learners on top of their lesson plans. Warning signs get missed simply because our teachers are stretched too thin.

Rights & Laws

Key Philippine laws and DepEd policies that protect students from bullying, safeguard your mental health, and hold schools accountable.

RA 10627

Republic Act 10627 (Anti-Bullying Act of 2013)

Requires all elementary and high schools to have an active Child Protection Committee (CPC). Schools must investigate bullying complaints, protect victims, and provide counseling for everyone involved.

What this law requires schools to do ↓
  • Active Child Protection Committee: Every school must set up a committee with teachers, parents, and student council reps to handle reports.
  • Protection from Retaliation: Strict rules protecting anyone who reports bullying or speaks up as a witness from being harassed.
  • Online Bullying Included: Covers cyberbullying even if it happens off-campus, as long as it affects students at school.
Read the full law ↗
RA 11036

Republic Act 11036 (Philippine Mental Health Act)

Requires schools to provide mental health awareness programs, fight stigma, and connect students to counseling services.

How this protects student mental health ↓
  • Counseling & Help: Schools must connect struggling students to licensed counselors and mental health professionals.
  • Protection Against Expulsion: Students cannot be expelled or suspended just because they are dealing with mental health struggles.
  • Teacher Training: Teachers must be trained to recognize early warning signs and know how to help safely.
Read the full law ↗
DO 40

DepEd Order No. 40, s. 2012 (Child Protection Policy)

Enforces strict zero tolerance for any form of child abuse, physical fights, exploitation, or bullying on school grounds.

What this zero-tolerance policy covers ↓
  • Zero Tolerance for Abuse: Strictly bans corporal punishment, verbal insults, or humiliation from both teachers and classmates.
  • Fast Action (Within 48 Hours): School heads must start looking into reported incidents within 48 hours.
  • Protecting Student Privacy: Strict rules keeping the identities of victims and those who report safe and confidential.

What We Can Do

01

Don't record or cheer on fights.

Crowding around with phone cameras or uploading brawl clips to TikTok and Facebook only fuels more violence and humiliates people further.

02

Never provoke someone at their limit.

If a classmate is having a breakdown, screaming, or shaking with anger, back away immediately, give them breathing room, and get an adult.

03

Speak up about threats and weapons.

If someone mentions wanting to end their life, threatens a classmate, or brings a blade or firearm to campus, tell a teacher, counselor, or school head right away.

How We Built This (6 ICT Phases)

01

Choosing Our Topic

We wanted to address a real issue affecting students around us. After discussing recent campus incidents, our group chose to focus on student mental health and school violence. We set up our Messenger GC and Trello board right away to stay organized.

02

Planning the Sections & Roles

We broke the site down into 6 practical tabs: Home, Real Cases, Mental Health, Laws, Hotlines, and About Us. Everyone took on roles based on their strengths, and we hopped on Google Meet to align on deliverables.

03

Designing in Canva

We sketched our layouts and draft mockups in Canva. We picked a dark editorial style with teal accents so the information feels clean, respectful, and easy to read on both phones and laptops.

04

Gathering Research & Assets

We set up our GitHub repo, gathered verified news articles, looked up Las Piñas emergency numbers, and collected our graduation completer portraits before coding.

05

Coding From Scratch

We wrote the website using clean HTML, CSS, and JavaScript without relying on heavy page builders. We focused on keeping it fast, making the search instant, and adding practical tools like one-tap dialing and a breathing exercise.

06

Testing on Phones & Submitting

We tested the site on multiple phones and laptops, checked every phone number link, fixed spacing and layout bugs, and compiled our screenshots for grading.
